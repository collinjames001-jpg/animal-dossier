About The Trumpeter Hornbill

  • Common Name: Trumpeter Hornbill
  • Endangerment Status: Least Concern
  • Phylum: Chordata
  • Class: Aves
  • Subclass: Neornithes
  • Order: Bucerotiformes
  • Family: Bucerotidae
  • Genus: Bycanistes
  • Size: 23 to 26 inches
  • markings: black-and-white plumage, featuring a glossy black head, neck, back, and wings, contrasted with a white belly and underparts
  • Habitat: tropical evergreen forests and tall deciduous woodlands of southeastern and central Africa
  • Diet: mostly fruits, alongside insects and small animal protein
  • Behaviors: highly social, noisy, and cavity-nesting omnivores
  • Comunication: loud, resonant vocalizations like wails, chirps, and whistles that sound remarkably like a human baby crying or a echoing horn
  • Life Span: 20 years on average
  • Predators: Large birds of prey, tree-dwelling carnivores, and snakes

The trumpeter hornbill is a medium sized brid with white and black feathers on their backs and bellys with white tips on their wings and tail feathers the beak on the trumpeter hornbill is quite large and is normally larger on the males the males have a purple and red color around its eyes and the females beak is yellowish at the base.
